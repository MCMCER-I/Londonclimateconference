The Climate Imperative

£9–£45 Billion — the capital cost of climate-resilience retrofitting London's commercial building stock.

With record summer temperatures breaching 40°C and the UK Government's "Heat Ready London" plan coming into force, adaptation is no longer an abstract CSR ambition — it is a pressing fiduciary and operational requirement.

While major infrastructure is planned, the acute bottleneck is business capability: corporate real estate, enterprise workplaces, and finance teams lack the operational blueprints and reporting know-how to comply with mandatory physical risk disclosures under GRI, CSRD, and IFRS S2.

This Forum convenes selected institutional leaders into focused working roundtables to bridge this capability deficit with tangible solutions.

Parallel Working Sessions

Two Working Roundtables

Delegates break into two dedicated, parallel chambers for 90 minutes of moderated, high-intensity problem solving led by recognized subject matter authorities.

Chamber A · Physical Assets & Retrofit 17 Selected Leaders

Heat-Resilient Buildings, Infrastructure & Skills

Addressing London's commercial asset retrofitting gap, passive cooling technologies, district heat mitigation, and the urgent green engineering skills pipeline.

Core Deliberation Prompts
Overheating Thresholds: Practical retrofitting of commercial office towers against CIBSE TM59/TM52 criteria.
Passive Engineering: Integration of external shading, green roofs, and facade ventilation over energy-intensive AC.
Capital Allocation: De-risking green retrofit financing with commercial banks and London authorities.
Workforce Capabilities: Scaling MEP engineering and facade installer apprenticeships across Greater London.
Lead Moderator · Chamber A
Prof. Kirk W. Junker
Vice Rector for Sustainability, University of Cologne
Key Output: Commercial Building Adaptation & Skills Playbook clauses
Chamber B · Enterprise ESG & Disclosure 18 Selected Leaders

Corporate Adaptation, Workforce & Climate Disclosure

Tackling corporate exposure to physical heat: employee heat-stress duty-of-care, supply chain business continuity, and quantitative physical risk reporting under GRI, CSRD, and IFRS S2.

Core Deliberation Prompts
Workforce Duty-of-Care: Operational guidelines for frontline, transport, and desk workers during severe heat alerts.
Productivity Economics: Quantifying lost employee hours and HVAC strain for enterprise CFOs.
Physical Climate Risk Reporting: Auditable metrics compliant with IFRS S2 and CSRD ESRS E1 standards.
Supply Chain Contingency: Critical supplier resilience and temperature-sensitive distribution in SE England.
Lead Moderator · Chamber B
Alessandra Vignola
Sustainability Academic & Consultant · GRI Certified
Key Output: Corporate Heat Adaptation Protocol & Disclosure Toolkit
